# Chick Shack, voice of the customer

**Source:** the 16 Google reviews on the live profile, read 2026-08-16. 5.0 average, every one
five stars. **All of them predate the review email** (newest is ~26 July; the email went live
10 Aug), so this is unprompted, organic praise from the shop's opening weeks.

**Why this file exists:** email copy that uses the customer's own words outperforms copy we invent
for them. Before writing any campaign, subject line or menu blurb, take the language from here
rather than from imagination. Update it when new reviews land.

---

## The words they actually use

**Chicken**
> "fresh, succulent chicken" · "the chicken is the best about fresh n succulent" ·
> "food was very fresh, cooked to perfection" · "The food was fresh and delicious"

**Fresh** is the single most repeated word across the set. It belongs in subject lines and item
descriptions, and it is not a word we have used anywhere in the emails so far.

**The chicken fillet burger, compared to KFC, twice, unprompted**
> "the chicken fillet burger tastes so similar to a KFC mini fillet" ·
> "got the chicken fillet burger, 10/10"

Two separate strangers reached for the same comparison. That is a positioning gift: the item has a
reference point people already understand. **Do not put the KFC name in marketing copy** (trademark,
and it flatters a competitor), but it tells us the fillet burger is the hero item to lead with.

**Chips**
> "perfectly cooked chips" · "peri chips are banging" · "chips are amazing too!! perfectly cooked
> and worth the price"

Chips come up nearly as often as the chicken. Relevant to the OI-82 attach-rate finding: 50 of 67
orders under £25 had no side at all, yet chips are what people rave about. **The upsell is the thing
they already love.**

**Portions and price**
> "generous portion sizes" · "reasonably priced" · "worth the price"
> Price per person recorded by reviewers: £1-10 on five reviews, £10-20 on two.

**Staff and place**
> "welcoming, friendly, knowledgeable and have a great sense of humour" ·
> "the welcoming, friendly staff and the spotless, tidy premises" · "the place is spotless" ·
> "The takeaway is very clean and tidy" · "great staff" · "Really helpful on the phone"

**Community**
> "Certainly a brilliant addition to the community" · "A lovely new addition to garelochhead" ·
> "Great to have somewhere like this in the village"

Three people independently framed it as *the village gaining something*. That is the emotional
register for the "from behind the counter" email, and it is the one thing a chain cannot claim.

**Delivery and service**
> "the food was delivered promptly" · "No wait"

**Overcoming a bad expectation**
> "Don't know why people were so negative about this place opening. We have been a couple of times
> now and everything we have had has been phenomenal."

Worth knowing there was local scepticism before opening, and that the food converted it.

## The governing direction, set by Malik 2026-08-16

> "The village tone, language and way we draft emails and future promotional material should be
> navigated and directed by this village-centric theme and tonality."

**This is the standing brief for all Chick Shack copy**, emails and promotional material alike, not
a note on one template. It is grounded in the reviews rather than invented: three customers
independently framed the shop as the village gaining something, unprompted, and it is the one claim
a national chain structurally cannot make.

**Two boundary conditions, or it backfires:**

1. ⚠️ **The delivery radius is much bigger than the village.** Per the OI-76 research the shop
   delivers across 20-25 miles of rural Argyll: Rhu, Rahane, Shore Road and beyond. A customer in
   Rhu does not live in Garelochhead. **Treat it as a register, not a word.** Local, independent,
   run by people you can actually phone, part of the community. Say "the village" only when the
   audience really is Garelochhead; say "round here" for the wider radius.
2. ⚠️ **It is a tone, not a slogan, and it must never crowd out the food.** "Fresh" and the fillet
   burger do the selling; village does the belonging. Copy that opens with community warmth every
   time and buries the chicken gets twee quickly and stops shifting orders.

## How to use this in email

1. **Village register throughout**, per the brief above, within those two limits.
2. **Lead with "fresh"**, because they do, and we currently do not use the word at all.
3. **The chicken fillet burger is the hero item.** If a template needs one dish, use that.
4. **Chips are the natural attach**, and reviewers already love them. An add-a-side prompt is not a
   sell, it is agreeing with them.
5. ⚠️ **Never quote a named reviewer in a marketing email without asking them.** Use the language,
   not the attribution. Google reviews are public but a customer's name in a promo is a different
   thing from a customer's name on a review, and it is their call, not ours.

## Where this does NOT reach
The transactional order emails (received, accepted, on the way) stay plain and factual. They land in
**Primary** precisely because they read as a transaction rather than a solicitation, and warming
them up with village copy risks pushing them into Promotions, where a customer can miss "your order
is accepted, 40 minutes". **Tone applies to marketing. Leave the order emails alone.**
